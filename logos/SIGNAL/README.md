# SIGNAL. by SIRYUS — approved visual lockup exports

These exports use the **latest visually approved serif lockup with small italic/script `by SIRYUS`**, not the previously rejected geometric sans-serif iterations. The circular red period and three-path emblem are preserved.

## Primary assets
- `signal-horizontal-dark.png` and `signal-horizontal-light.png`: cropped original AI-rendered approved lockups, with their backgrounds.
- `signal-horizontal-transparent-for-dark.png`: transparency derived from the original black-background image. Use **on dark backgrounds**; its pale lettering is not suited to light backgrounds.
- `signal-stacked-transparent-for-dark.png`: layout assembled using crops of the original approved emblem and wordmark; a convenience variant, not a separately approved composition.
- `signal-symbol-transparent.png`: original three-path emblem cropped and background-isolated.
- `signal-symbol-white.png` / `signal-symbol-graphite.png`: one-color silhouette versions.
- `signal-favicon-*.png` and `favicon.ico`: raster icon derivatives.
- `REFERENCE-*`: complete original AI renders from which the kit was derived.

## Important quality limits
The source is an AI-generated **raster** image, not editable original vector artwork. The background removal may retain minor antialiasing halos, and it is **not a faithful SVG vector master**. The image typography cannot be confidently named as a specific font. Before professional print, trademark submission, or large signage, commission/manual-trace a clean vector master against the approved reference. Do not claim a particular font family unless the lettering is actually typeset using it.

## Site usage
Copy files under `public/logos/signal/` and use `/logos/signal/signal-horizontal-dark.png` for dark sections, or the corresponding light image for light sections. For a true transparent logo on light backgrounds, a professionally rebuilt layered/vector master is recommended.

## Colors (approximate from approved direction)
Graphite `#0B0B0C`, Warm stone `#F3EDE4`, Red `#E32620`; actual raster pixels vary due to highlights/antialiasing.
